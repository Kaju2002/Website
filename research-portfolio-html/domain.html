Literature survey

What existing research has done

Key previous studies reviewed for each component, what they contributed, and what they leave unsolved.

Santhosh S · IT22113740

Earlier work treats fake job detection as an English-only, real-or-fake classification problem. Most studies use the EMSCAD dataset with machine learning or deep learning classifiers and report accuracy above 94%, but very few are deployed, and almost none explain their predictions or give users a graded risk level.

Study Contribution Limitation
Dutta & Bandyopadhyay (2020) Compared seven ML classifiers on EMSCAD; Random Forest reached 98.27% accuracy. Little NLP beyond basic features and no handling of class imbalance.
Pillai (2023) Bidirectional LSTM with word embeddings, reaching 98.71% accuracy. No interpretability and no risk levels for end users.
Allam et al. (2025) Tested six class-balancing strategies across four classifiers. Research prototype only, with no user interface or explanations.
Pangare et al. (2026) JobScamShield: ML classification plus company checks through the GST registry and Glassdoor. Works only in India and real-time API calls add latency.
Singh et al. (2025) Heuristic signals such as free email domains and scam keywords with Logistic Regression. No deep semantic understanding from transformer models.
Sathwika et al. (2024) Django web app with several ML classifiers; Logistic Regression reached 98% accuracy. English only, with no retraining pipeline or explanations for users.

What is missing

All published fake job detection research covers English only and returns a binary verdict. No study combines transformer models with SHAP/LIME explanations in this domain, and no trilingual (English, Sinhala, Tamil) dataset exists from real Sri Lankan job portals.

Bandara H.M.N.T · IT22251664

Researchers have applied machine learning, NLP and data mining to detect fraudulent job postings. Most studies analyse the job advertisement itself (description, title, salary and posting attributes) with classifiers such as Random Forest, Logistic Regression and SVM, rather than the credibility of the employer behind it.

Study Contribution Limitation
Vidros et al. (2017) Introduced the public EMSCAD dataset and analysed the characteristics of fraudulent job posts. Focuses on advert content, not the legitimacy of the employer behind the post.
Alghamdi & Alharby (2019) Intelligent ML model for recruitment fraud detection using job metadata and text. Remains limited to job advertisement analysis.
Dutta & Bandyopadhyay (2020) ML classification of job text and metadata to identify fraud patterns. Employer identity and company credibility are not considered.
Allam et al. (2025) Data balancing with ML classifiers improved fake job detection accuracy. Uses advertisement data rather than employer credibility.
Singh et al. (2025) Showed the strong performance of Random Forest in detecting fake jobs. Relies heavily on textual job features.
Ansar & Hussain (2025) Survey showing how job scams reduce trust in online job platforms. Proposes no technical model for detecting fraudulent employers.

What is missing

Employer credibility evidence (official registers, reputation and scam-report platforms, website signals) is rarely combined, and most systems give only a binary label with no explanation of why an employer is suspicious.

Kajanthan U · IT22224002

Most research classifies job advertisements as fake or real, usually on the EMSCAD dataset. A second strand detects scam messages on SMS or WhatsApp with BERT-based models, and a third uses OCR to read scam text from screenshots. Behavioural studies have mapped the manipulation tactics scammers use, but these findings have not been built into an automated detector.

Study Contribution Limitation
Alghamdi & Alharby (2019) SVM and Random Forest on EMSCAD, reaching 97.41% accuracy on fake job ads. Analyses only the advert text, not recruiter–candidate conversations.
Taneja et al. (2025) Fraud-BERT, a fine-tuned transformer reaching an F1 score of 0.93 on EMSCAD. Limited to job posts; no protection once chats move to private channels.
Jain et al. (2025) BERT-based SMS smishing detection that outperformed keyword-based classifiers. Single platform only, with a binary scam or legitimate label.
Shinde et al. (2024) OCR to extract scam text from screenshots without platform API access. Traditional classifiers after OCR miss paraphrased scam expressions.
Anagha et al. (2026) Identified four job scam tactics: urgency, FOMO, sunk-cost influence and social proof. Purely theoretical; never built into a real-time detection system.
Tyszkiewicz & Noor (2025) SafeChat: cumulative risk scores for conversation-level scam detection on SMS. General scams on SMS only, without identifying the manipulation tactic.

What is missing

No existing system analyses the recruiter–candidate conversation where money is actually lost, or identifies which manipulation tactic is being used, across multiple platforms and in a way non-technical job seekers can understand.

Methmi N P C S · IT22188618

Job recommendation research matches job seekers to jobs by skills, experience and preferences, using collaborative, content-based and hybrid filtering. These approaches optimise relevance and accuracy but never check whether a job posting is safe, and existing risk- or trust-aware recommenders target other domains rather than recruitment fraud.

Study Contribution Limitation
Zhang et al. (2014) Job recommender using user-based and item-based collaborative filtering. Cold-start problems and no check on whether postings are legitimate.
Nadar et al. Content-based recommender matching profiles to jobs with TF-IDF and cosine similarity. Considers relevance only, not the safety of postings.
Çano & Morisio (2017) Reviewed hybrid recommenders that tackle sparsity and cold start. Focused on accuracy and ignores fraudulent job postings.
Bouneffouf (2013) DRARS, a risk-aware recommender using a contextual bandit for situational risk. General recommendation risk only, not fraud on job platforms.
Masrom et al. (2018) Trust-aware recommender using trust relationships between users. Covers trust between users, not fraudulent postings or employers.
Madanchian & Taherdoost (2020) Described TOPSIS for ranking options by distance from ideal solutions. General decision-making; not designed to include fraud risk.

What is missing

No existing system brings skill matching and multiple fraud signals (fake job, employer legitimacy and scam communication) together with a ranking method, so jobs are never ranked by both relevance and safety.

Project scope

From research gap to solution

How the project moves from what is missing in existing work, to the problem we address, to the system we propose.

01

Research Gap

Existing research and tools tackle recruitment fraud in isolated pieces. They focus mainly on the text of job advertisements and treat fraud detection and job recommendation as separate problems.

  • Fake job detection models work only in English and analyse only the job advert text, ignoring Sinhala and Tamil speakers, the employer behind the post and the conversation that follows.
  • Employer credibility indicators such as website domain consistency, recruiter email alignment and LinkedIn presence are rarely analysed systematically.
  • Scam message detectors work on a single platform (SMS or WhatsApp), give only a "scam or not" label, and don't identify the manipulation tactics scammers use.
  • Job recommendation systems rank jobs by skill relevance alone and never consider fraud risk.
02

Research Problem

How can job seekers be protected across the entire recruitment journey (the job post, the employer and the recruiter's messages) and be guided towards jobs that are both relevant and safe, with explanations they can understand?

  • Fraudulent job posts look professional and get past basic moderation on popular job platforms.
  • Scammers hide behind fake or impersonated employer identities that job seekers can't easily verify.
  • Financial harm happens in private chats on WhatsApp, Telegram and SMS, through tactics like urgency pressure and fear of missing out, where no protection exists.
  • A plain "fake or real" label without a reason gives non-technical users little to trust or act on.
03

Proposed Solution

An AI-powered mobile ecosystem of four integrated components that checks every stage of recruitment and combines their results into a single safety score, which is then used to recommend safer jobs.

  • Fake Job Post Detection: flags fraudulent job posts in English, Sinhala and Tamil with XLM-RoBERTa, giving a Low / Medium / High suspicion score and SHAP/LIME explanations.
  • Employer Legitimacy Verification: scores employer credibility with a Random Forest and multi-source evidence fusion, and explains each result through an evidence report and score breakdown.
  • BSTD: detects manipulation tactics in recruiter conversations in real time using DistilBERT, with OCR to read screenshots from any platform.
  • Fraud-Aware Recommendation: combines all the risk signals into one score and ranks jobs by both skill match and safety, using the Entropy Weight Method and TOPSIS.
Research objectives

Objectives by component

Each team member owns one component of the system. Every component has its own novelty, main objective and sub-objectives.

Overall main objective

To design and develop an AI-powered mobile ecosystem that detects recruitment fraud across job posts, employers and recruiter conversations, and uses the combined fraud risk to recommend job opportunities that are both relevant and safe, with clear explanations for job seekers.

01

Multilingual Fake Job Post Detection

Santhosh S · IT22113740

Novelty

The first fake job detection system for English, Sinhala and Tamil. It is trained on a new trilingual dataset built from real Sri Lankan job portals, gives a three-tier Low / Medium / High suspicion score instead of a plain real-or-fake verdict, and explains every prediction with SHAP and LIME.

Main objective

To design, develop and evaluate a multilingual AI-powered mobile application that detects fake job postings in Sinhala, Tamil and English using XLM-RoBERTa, assigns a three-tier suspicion score and provides SHAP/LIME-based explanations, achieving an F1-score of at least 90% across all three languages.

Sub-objectives

  1. 1.Build a trilingual fake job dataset by scraping topjobs.lk and ikman.lk and labelling each post as fake or legitimate (Cohen's Kappa ≥ 0.80).
  2. 2.Fine-tune XLM-RoBERTa on the dataset with SMOTE-based class balancing to reach an F1-score of at least 90% in every language.
  3. 3.Design a three-tier suspicion score combining model probability, risky keywords, missing fields and free email domains.
  4. 4.Integrate SHAP and LIME to explain the top features behind each prediction, validated with at least 30 participants.
  5. 5.Develop a React Native mobile app with a REST API backend, supporting all three languages in the interface.
  6. 6.Evaluate the full system on a held-out test set and through a user study with at least 50 participants.
02

Employer Legitimacy Verification

Bandara H.M.N.T · IT22251664

Novelty

Verifies the employer behind a job post, not just the advertisement text. The component checks a company against Sri Lankan official sources (Colombo Stock Exchange, Registrar of Companies, Central Bank, Board of Investment, Insurance Regulatory Commission and the Specified Business Enterprises list), its reputation on review and professional platforms, and the signals on its own website. A Random Forest model and a weighted fusion score combine this evidence into a 0–100 score and a Low, Medium or High-risk band. Every result comes with an evidence report that links each statement to its source and shows how many points each layer contributed. A missing registry entry is treated as “not confirmed” and not as proof of fraud, so genuine small businesses are not unfairly penalised.

Main objective

To design and develop an explainable, multi-source Employer Legitimacy Verification service that combines registry, reputation and website evidence to classify employers by risk, with weights and risk thresholds justified by held-out evidence, and to integrate it with the job platform and the risk-aware job recommendation component.

Sub-objectives

  1. 1.Review related work on employer verification and identify the gaps, such as the lack of Sri Lankan registry coverage, explainability and tested weights.
  2. 2.Design a multi-source evidence pipeline with registry, reputation and website layers and 14 structured features.
  3. 3.Build a labelled dataset of 1,514 employers and train a Random Forest classifier, evaluated on a domain-grouped train, validation and test split (test accuracy 0.917, F1 0.935, AUC 0.973).
  4. 4.Define the fusion score and determine its weights by experiment, and report the cost of keeping the score explainable (fraud recall 78.1% for the explainable score against 93.3% for the best weights).
  5. 5.Define Low, Medium and High-risk bands that keep fraud out of the Low band (none of the 107 Low-band test employers was fraudulent).
  6. 6.Expose the component as a FastAPI service, verified by 38 automated tests, that screens company registrations (Low and Medium verified automatically, High sent to an administrator with the evidence report) and supplies the employer risk to the recommendation component.
  7. 7.Evaluate usefulness with users in the final acceptance sessions.
03

Scam Conversation & Manipulation Tactic Detection (BSTD)

Kajanthan U · IT22224002

Novelty

The first system to detect psychological manipulation tactics (urgency pressure, FOMO, sunk-cost influence and social proof) in real-time recruiter–candidate conversations. It turns the behavioural findings of Anagha et al. into a working detector with plain-language warnings, and supports three input modes: a built-in recruiter chat analysed as messages arrive, pasted messages from WhatsApp, Telegram, SMS, LinkedIn or email, and uploaded screenshots read with OCR.

Main objective

To design, develop and evaluate a Behaviour-Inspired Semantic Tactic Detection (BSTD) system that analyses recruiter–candidate messages with NLP, detects manipulation tactics in real time and delivers explainable warnings, achieving an F1 score of at least 0.90 and a SUS score of 70 or above.

Sub-objectives

  1. 1.Review existing literature on recruitment fraud, scam communication and manipulation tactics to identify research gaps.
  2. 2.Build and annotate a multi-platform dataset of recruiter–candidate scam messages with scam and tactic labels.
  3. 3.Implement a TF-IDF and Logistic Regression baseline to benchmark performance.
  4. 4.Fine-tune a DistilBERT multi-label classifier to identify the four manipulation tactics.
  5. 5.Develop an OCR screenshot pipeline for cross-platform detection without platform API access.
  6. 6.Deploy a mobile app with a FastAPI REST API that delivers real-time results and plain-language warnings.
04

Fraud-Aware Job Recommendation & Ranking

Methmi N P C S · IT22188618

Novelty

Combines skill relevance with fraud risk from all three detection modules. Risk indicators are aggregated with the Entropy Weight Method into a safety score, and jobs are ranked with TOPSIS so recommendations are both relevant and safe.

Main objective

To develop a risk-aware job recommendation and ranking model that integrates skill matching and fraud risk analysis to recommend relevant and safer job opportunities for job seekers.

Sub-objectives

  1. 1.Match user profiles with job descriptions using TF-IDF and cosine similarity.
  2. 2.Convert results from fake job, employer legitimacy and scam communication detection into risk indicators.
  3. 3.Calculate an overall risk score for each job using the Entropy Weight Method.
  4. 4.Convert the risk score into a safety score representing job trustworthiness.
  5. 5.Rank candidate jobs with TOPSIS using both skill match and safety scores.
  6. 6.Generate final recommendations that balance relevance and safety.

01 Methodology

Multilingual Fake Job Post Detection

Santhosh S · IT22113740

Following Design Science Research, job posts scraped from Sri Lankan portals are labelled and used to fine-tune XLM-RoBERTa. A weighted suspicion score and SHAP/LIME explanations are returned to the mobile app, with Tesseract OCR handling image-based posts such as WhatsApp screenshots.

Technologies

Model
Python Hugging Face XLM-RoBERTa
Explainability
SHAP LIME
App & infrastructure
FastAPI React Native AWS MongoDB Atlas

Process

  1. 01 Trilingual dataset scraping (Scrapy, Selenium) and manual labelling
  2. 02 Class balancing with SMOTE-ENN
  3. 03 XLM-RoBERTa fine-tuning for fake or legitimate classification
  4. 04 Three-tier suspicion scoring with fuzzy logic (Low / Medium / High)
  5. 05 SHAP and LIME explanations in the user's language
  6. 06 Evaluation: F1 ≥ 90%, ROC-AUC, SUS > 70 user study
02 Methodology

Employer Legitimacy Verification

Bandara H.M.N.T · IT22251664

Evidence about an employer is collected from Sri Lankan registers, review platforms and the company website, turned into 14 structured features, and scored by a Random Forest. The model score is combined with the evidence of each layer by a weighted fusion into a 0–100 score and a Low, Medium or High-risk band, and the result is returned with an evidence report through a REST service used by the mobile app and the platform.

Technologies

Data
Pandas NumPy
Model
Python scikit-learn joblib
Evidence collection
Requests BeautifulSoup Selenium duckduckgo-search python-whois
Service
FastAPI Uvicorn Pydantic
Testing
pytest
App and platform
React Native (Expo) Node.js backend MongoDB

Process

  1. 01 Review related work and identify the gaps in employer verification.
  2. 02 Design the three evidence layers (registry, reputation, website) and build a labelled dataset of 1,514 employers (978 genuine and 536 fraudulent) from public sources.
  3. 03 Engineer 14 structured features and train a Random Forest on a domain-grouped train, validation and test split.
  4. 04 Determine the fusion weights and the risk-band boundaries by experimenting on the validation data, then evaluate once on the test data with accuracy, precision, recall, F1, AUC and confidence intervals.
  5. 05 Build the evidence report and expose the component as a FastAPI service, verified with 38 automated unit tests and scenario tests.
  6. 06 Integrate the service with the platform for company registration screening and with the recommendation component, and validate it in user acceptance sessions.
03 Methodology

Scam Conversation & Manipulation Tactic Detection (BSTD)

Kajanthan U · IT22224002

Following Design Science Research, a fine-tuned DistilBERT model performs scam classification and multi-label tactic detection on messages from a built-in chat, pasted text or OCR-processed screenshots, with an explanation engine generating plain-language warnings.

  4. 04 OCR screenshot pipeline with OpenCV and Tesseract

04 Methodology

Fraud-Aware Job Recommendation & Ranking

Methmi N P C S · IT22188618

Using a Design Science approach, candidate jobs are found through skill matching, their fraud risk is aggregated from the other three components, and the TOPSIS multi-criteria method ranks them by skill match and safety.

Technologies

Pandas NumPy
Model
Python Scikit-learn
App & infrastructure

Process

  1. 01 Skill matching with TF-IDF and cosine similarity
  2. 02 Candidate job selection from top matches
  3. 03 Collecting risk indicators from the three detection modules
  4. 04 Risk score calculation with the Entropy Weight Method
  5. 05 Safety score conversion (1 − risk score)
  6. 06 TOPSIS ranking and safe job recommendations
